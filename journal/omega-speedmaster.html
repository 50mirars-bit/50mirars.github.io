THE MOONWATCH • EST. 1957

The Watch That Reached the Moon

Before it became one of the most celebrated watches in history, the Omega Speedmaster was simply a professional chronograph built for precision. Then NASA tested it against the impossible—and it became the first watch worn on the Moon.

THE EDITOR'S NOTE

More Than a Chronograph

The Omega Speedmaster represents one of humanity's greatest achievements. Introduced in 1957 for motorsport, it unexpectedly became essential equipment for astronauts after surviving NASA's demanding qualification tests in 1965. That remarkable journey transformed the Speedmaster into the legendary Moonwatch worn during every Apollo lunar landing.

NASA QUALIFICATION • 1965

When Precision Met Space

NASA exposed several chronographs to brutal testing that included extreme temperatures, violent vibration, powerful shocks and rapid pressure changes. Only one watch completed every challenge successfully—the Omega Speedmaster.

That qualification changed watchmaking forever. Without being designed specifically for space, the Speedmaster earned a place beside astronauts as official equipment for manned missions.

Its simple black dial, three-register chronograph layout and tachymeter bezel proved that exceptional engineering often begins with exceptional clarity.

"A watch built for Earth became the first watch worn on the Moon."
— 50 MIRARS JOURNAL
16 JULY 1969 • KENNEDY SPACE CENTER

The Rocket That Carried History

At 13:32 UTC, the Saturn V lifted Apollo 11 toward the Moon. On board were Neil Armstrong, Buzz Aldrin and Michael Collins—each wearing an officially issued Omega Speedmaster, already trusted by NASA after surviving its brutal qualification tests four years earlier.

THE MOONWATCH MOMENT

When Time Left Earth

Neil Armstrong's Speedmaster remained inside the Lunar Module as a backup timing instrument after an onboard timer issue, making Buzz Aldrin's Speedmaster the first watch worn on the Moon.

Strapped over the outside of his spacesuit, the Speedmaster became part of one of the most recognizable images in human history.

That moment transformed a Swiss chronograph into an enduring symbol of exploration, courage and precision.

"One small step for man, one giant leap for mankind."
— NEIL ARMSTRONG • APOLLO 11
APRIL 1970 • APOLLO 13

The Mission That Saved the Moonwatch

When an oxygen tank exploded aboard Apollo 13, the Moon landing became a desperate fight for survival. With onboard systems failing, astronauts relied on their Omega Speedmasters to time a critical 14-second engine burn that helped place the spacecraft on a safe trajectory home.

That extraordinary moment transformed the Speedmaster from a Moonwatch into one of history's greatest survival instruments.

THE HEART OF THE SPEEDMASTER

Calibre 321

The original Moonwatch was powered by Omega's legendary Calibre 321—a manually wound column-wheel chronograph admired for its precision and reliability.

Its smooth operation, elegant architecture and remarkable durability made it perfectly suited for the demanding conditions of space exploration.

Today, collectors regard the Calibre 321 as one of the greatest chronograph movements ever created.

NASA RECOGNITION

The Silver Snoopy Award

In 1970, NASA honored Omega with its prestigious Silver Snoopy Award in recognition of the Speedmaster's contribution to the safe return of Apollo 13.

Presented by astronauts themselves, the award remains one of Omega's proudest achievements and symbolizes the extraordinary partnership between watchmaking and space exploration.

MASTER CHRONOMETER

The Moonwatch Evolves

In 2021, Omega introduced a new generation of the Speedmaster Professional that honored the original Moonwatch while embracing modern engineering through the Master Chronometer standard.

Calibre 3861

The modern Moonwatch is powered by the Co-Axial Master Chronometer Calibre 3861, combining exceptional precision with impressive resistance to magnetic fields up to 15,000 gauss.

Despite these advancements, Omega preserved the familiar asymmetrical case, black dial and manually wound experience that made the original Speedmaster legendary.

It remains unmistakably the Moonwatch—only better prepared for the demands of the modern world.
