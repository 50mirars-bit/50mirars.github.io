02
ORIGINS

DAYTONA BEACH TO LE MANS

Long before the Cosmograph Daytona existed as a physical timepiece, Rolex was entwined with high-speed motor racing. In the 1930s, Sir Malcolm Campbell set land-speed records at Daytona Beach wearing a Rolex Oyster.

When Daytona International Speedway opened in 1959, Rolex became its Official Timekeeper, cementing a legacy built on extreme endurance.

03
CHAPTER I — DESIGN REVOLUTION

THE BIRTH OF REF. 6239

Prior to 1963, chronographs printed tachymeter scales directly on the dial face. Rolex shifted the scale onto the external bezel, dramatically improving legibility for drivers timing laps at speed.

1963

Manual-Wind Era

Powered by manual Valjoux movements with inverse sub-dial colorways.

04
CHAPTER II — THE AUCTION MYTHOS

THE EXOTIC "PAUL NEWMAN" DIAL

Produced by dial maker Jean-Singer & Cie, "Exotic Dials" featured crosshair sub-dials and art-deco block font numerals. Initially slow sellers, these rare configurations later became the most sought-after vintage watches on Earth.

Their scarcity during the 1960s and 1970s laid the foundation for modern watch collecting culture.

05
RECORD BREAKING GRAIL

$17.75 MILLION LEGEND

"Drive Carefully Me" — Engraved on the caseback of Paul Newman’s Ref. 6239 by Joanne Woodward.

In October 2017, Paul Newman's personal wristwatch set a world record, solidifying the Daytona's status in horological history.

06
CHAPTER III — AUTOMATIC ERA

REF. 16520: ZENITH CALIBRE 4030

In 1988, Rolex motorized the Daytona with self-winding mechanics. Based on the Zenith El Primero, Rolex modified over 50% of the movement components to create the highly durable Calibre 4030.

Case sizes expanded to 40mm, introducing crown guards and sapphire crystals to the collection.

07
CHAPTER IV — IN-HOUSE AUTONOMY

CALIBRE 4130 (YEAR 2000)

Entering the 21st century, Rolex introduced Ref. 116520 featuring a vertical clutch vertical-clutch chronograph engine engineered entirely in-house, offering a 72-hour power reserve.

The vertical clutch architecture eliminated hand-jitter upon starting chronograph timing.

08
METALS
CHAPTER V — METALLURGICAL SCIENCE

OYSTERSTEEL & EVEROSE GOLD

Rolex utilizes 904L stainless steel—branded Oystersteel—for exceptional resistance to harsh environments and superior polish sheen.

Precious metal models feature in-house cast 18ct Everose gold, blended with platinum to preserve color permanently.

09
CASE ARCHITECTURE

TRIPLOCK SYSTEM & OYSTERCASE

The Monobloc middle case features screw-down pushers and a Triplock winding crown with four waterproof gaskets, guaranteeing 100 meters (330 feet) water resistance.

Rugged screw-down pushers prevent inadvertent underwater activation while preserving the silhouette.

10
CHAPTER VI — MODERN HOROLOGY

CERACHROM & CALIBRE 4131

Modern Daytona models utilize high-tech Cerachrom ceramic bezels that are scratchproof and corrosion-resistant, updated with thin metallic outer borders in Ref. 126500LN.

Calibre 4131 incorporates the Chronergy escapement for optimized energy efficiency.
