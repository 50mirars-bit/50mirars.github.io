02
ORIGINS

DAYTONA BEACH TO LE MANS

Long before the Cosmograph Daytona existed as a physical watch, Rolex was deeply entwined with speed. In the 1930s, Sir Malcolm Campbell set five land-speed records at Daytona Beach, Florida, wearing an Oyster watch.

When the Daytona International Speedway opened in 1959, Rolex became its Official Timekeeper. By 1963, Rolex introduced Reference 6239, explicitly named the "Cosmograph" and soon updated with "Daytona" on the dial.

03
CHAPTER I — ARCHITECTURAL INNOVATION

Prior to Reference 6239, chronographs typically printed the tachymeter scale directly on the dial surface, cluttering the face. Rolex revolutionized racing ergonomics by moving the tachymeter scale onto the bezel itself.

This simple shift allowed drivers to instantly read speeds up to 300 units per hour (later expanded to 400) without losing visual focus on elapsed race times.

1963

The Inaugural Year

Reference 6239 debuts, equipped with manual-wind Valjoux 72 movements and high-contrast sub-dials.

"By positioning the tachymeter outside the crystal, Rolex created a new design language for performance timekeeping."

04
CHAPTER II — THE AUCTION MYTHOS

THE "PAUL NEWMAN" EXOTIC DIAL

In the late 1960s, Rolex offered specialized "Exotic Dials" manufactured by Jean-Singer & Cie. Characterized by art-deco subdial font numerals, step-down outer tracks, and small square hash marks, these dials were initially commercial failures.

Customers overwhelmingly preferred standard monochrome dials. As a result, Rolex produced very few exotic dial variants during the manual-wind production run between 1963 and 1988.

05
RECORD BREAKING GRAIL

REFERENCE 6239

"Drive Carefully Me" — Inscribed on the caseback of Paul Newman’s Daytona by his wife Joanne Woodward before his first motor race.

When Paul Newman was photographed repeatedly wearing his exotic dial Reference 6239 during his professional racing endeavors, Italian collectors dubbed the design the "Paul Newman Daytona".

In October 2017, Newman's personal watch sold at auction for $17.75 million—making it one of the most valuable wristwatches ever sold in history.

06
CHAPTER VII — MODERN HOROLOGY

THE CERACHROM ERA

In 2016, Rolex introduced the Reference 116500LN featuring a monobloc Cerachrom bezel in high-tech ceramic. Scratchproof and immune to UV fading, it paid homage to the 1963 acrylic bezel while offering modern durability.

Updated in 2023 as Reference 126500LN with Calibre 4131, it features a Chronergy escapement and a redesigned case silhouette with thin metal bezel borders.
