02
ORIGINS

DAYTONA BEACH TO LE MANS

Long before the Cosmograph Daytona existed as a physical watch, Rolex was deeply entwined with speed. In the 1930s, Sir Malcolm Campbell set five land-speed records at Daytona Beach, Florida, wearing an Oyster watch.

When the Daytona International Speedway opened in 1959, Rolex became its Official Timekeeper. By 1963, Rolex introduced Reference 6239, explicitly named the "Cosmograph" and soon updated with "Daytona" on the dial.

03
CHAPTER I — ARCHITECTURAL INNOVATION

Prior to Reference 6239, chronographs typically printed the tachymeter scale directly on the dial surface, cluttering the face. Rolex revolutionized racing ergonomics by moving the tachymeter scale onto the bezel itself.

This simple shift allowed drivers to instantly read speeds up to 300 units per hour (later expanded to 400) without losing visual focus on elapsed race times.

1963

The Inaugural Year

Reference 6239 debuts, equipped with manual-wind Valjoux 72 movements and high-contrast sub-dials.

"By positioning the tachymeter outside the crystal, Rolex created a new design language for performance timekeeping."

04
CHAPTER II — THE AUCTION MYTHOS

THE "PAUL NEWMAN" EXOTIC DIAL

In the late 1960s, Rolex offered specialized "Exotic Dials" manufactured by Jean-Singer & Cie. Characterized by art-deco subdial font numerals, step-down outer tracks, and small square hash marks, these dials were initially commercial failures.

Customers overwhelmingly preferred standard monochrome dials. As a result, Rolex produced very few exotic dial variants during the manual-wind production run between 1963 and 1988.

05
RECORD BREAKING GRAIL

REFERENCE 6239

"Drive Carefully Me" — Inscribed on the caseback of Paul Newman’s Daytona by his wife Joanne Woodward before his first motor race.

When Paul Newman was photographed repeatedly wearing his exotic dial Reference 6239 during his professional racing endeavors, Italian collectors dubbed the design the "Paul Newman Daytona".

In October 2017, Newman's personal watch sold at auction for $17.75 million—making it one of the most valuable wristwatches ever sold in history.

06
CHAPTER III — THE AUTOMATIC REVOLUTION

REFERENCE 16520: THE ZENITH ERA

By the late 1980s, market demand shifted permanently to self-winding mechanical movements. In 1988, Rolex released the Reference 16520, introducing automatic winding to the Daytona family.

Rolex heavily modified the Zenith El Primero 400 caliber—replacing over 50% of the components, reducing the frequency from 36,000 vph to 28,800 vph, and fitting a Microstella balance wheel to create the Rolex Calibre 4030.

07
CHAPTER IV — COMPLETE IN-HOUSE AUTONOMY

CALIBRE 4130 (YEAR 2000)

At the dawn of the new millennium, Rolex launched Reference 116520 equipped with Calibre 4130—a fully in-house vertical clutch chronograph movement. By reducing the overall component count by 20%, Rolex increased reliability and extended the power reserve to 72 hours.

The implementation of a vertical clutch eliminated chronograph second hand flutter upon activation, allowing continuous precision timing.

08
METALS
CHAPTER V — METALLURGICAL SCIENCE

OYSTERSTEEL & EVEROSE GOLD

Unlike standard watchmakers using 316L stainless steel, Rolex manufactures the Daytona using 904L alloy—branded internally as Oystersteel. Extremely resistant to severe corrosion, 904L maintains an exceptional sheen when mirror-polished.

For precious metal models, Rolex casts its own gold in an in-house foundry. Proprietary 18ct Everrose gold adds platinum to preserve the warm pink hue indefinitely against exposure to chlorine and salt water.

09
HERMETIC SEALING

TRIPLOCK CROWN & OYSTERCASE

The Daytona’s Monobloc middle case is stamped from a solid block of Oystersteel or gold. Screw-down pushers and a Triplock winding crown—featuring four seals—ensure hermetic water resistance down to 100 meters (330 feet).

Screw-down chronograph pushers guard against accidental activation underwater while cementing the watch’s muscular, utilitarian silhouette.

10
CHAPTER VI — ART OF THE DIAL

CHROMALIGHT & DIAL VARIANTS

Every Rolex dial is designed in-house to guarantee maximum readability. The hour markers and hands are crafted from 18ct gold to prevent tarnishing over decades of ownership.

Modern Daytona models feature Chromalight luminescent display—a photo-luminescent material emitting a long-lasting blue glow that remains visible twice as long as standard phosphorescent coatings.

11
EXCEPTIONAL MATERIALS

950 PLATINUM & ICE BLUE

To mark the 50th anniversary of the Cosmograph Daytona in 2013, Rolex introduced Reference 116506 in solid 950 platinum paired with an exclusive Ice Blue dial and chestnut brown Cerachrom bezel.

Ice Blue dials are reserved strictly for Rolex platinum timepieces, designating the pinnacle of the manufacture’s catalog.

12
CHAPTER VII — MODERN HOROLOGY

THE CERACHROM ERA

In 2016, Rolex introduced the Reference 116500LN featuring a monobloc Cerachrom bezel in high-tech ceramic. Scratchproof and immune to UV fading, it paid homage to the 1963 acrylic bezel while offering modern durability.

Updated in 2023 as Reference 126500LN with Calibre 4131, it features a Chronergy escapement and a redesigned case silhouette with thin metal bezel borders.
